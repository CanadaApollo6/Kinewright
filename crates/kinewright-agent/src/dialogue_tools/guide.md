Keep the speaker's meaning, the order of the exchange, qualifications and changes of mind. Preserve emphatic repetition and the other person's reply or reaction.

An abandoned start can go when the next take completes the thought. Remove a filler when the line flows better without it; keep it when it expresses hesitation or holds the speaker's turn. Shorten a pause that merely delays the next line, leaving room to think, react and breathe.

A barely audible aside usually goes. A deliberate whisper or a quiet reply needed to follow the exchange stays. These are preferences, not rules.

If a cut could change the meaning or lose a reaction, leave the passage. Tell the person what is at stake and what you recommend. Their stated preference overrides these defaults. Use the sounding-cut overrule only when the person asked for that cut.

After cutting, read the check. Deal with each flag, or explain why it should stand.
